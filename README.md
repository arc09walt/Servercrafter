# Servercrafter

**The Server Builder Guider. It doesn't build your server for you —
it walks beside you while you build it, one honest stage at a
time, until the server is standing and you hold the receipt.**

> **It's your server and you can reach it from anywhere —
> your own cloud, on your own machine, no landlord except
> yourself.**

Most ways of getting a home server hand you one of two things: a
landlord (somebody else's machine, somebody else's terms), or an
autopilot (a program that does everything behind a curtain and
leaves you owning a server you don't understand). Servercrafter
is the third thing: **a road**. Three acts, in plain words,
that you walk with your own hands — the guide prepares, it
points, it checks your work at every stage, and it writes the
receipt. The presses are yours. That's the whole design, and
it's deliberate: a server you built is a server you can fix at
3 a.m.

## What the walk is

- **One file. Double-click it.** The guide is a single
  self-contained page that opens in any browser. No install, no
  account, nothing it phones home to — ever.
- **Three acts: The House, The Sidewalk, The Street Door.**
  Act 1 lights your server inside your house in twelve stages;
  Act 2 walks it out to the street through its own tunnel door;
  Act 3 is the keeping of it — two clicks, and what to do when
  the door is closed. Each stage says what you're doing and why,
  in first-timer language (every piece of jargon is translated
  where it first appears). Where a command is needed, it's a
  complete copy-button block — never a blank for you to fill in,
  never a value you have to invent.
- **A stage can't close until its knock is satisfied.** The
  guide checks each step before it lets you move on. Six
  **Ambush Doors** stand ready for the things that go wrong
  mid-walk — the silent firewall, the network that calls itself
  Public, the phone that won't show the board — each with its
  plain-words way out.
- **Your keys stay yours.** The guide never sees, holds, or
  asks for your operator key or any password. There is no box
  to type one into. It can't leak what it never touches.
- **A receipt at the end.** What you did, in the order you did
  it, with a printable page up front: **"Why your server is
  safe"** — five plain lines, each tied to something you
  personally did with your own hands.
- **One server per walk.** Finished and want a second? Press
  **Build another server** and the walk starts spotless; your
  finished walks keep their receipts on a Past receipts shelf.

## The proof

This guide isn't a theory. The recipe it walks is a real server
— **the porch** — and a real porch built on it is standing
today:

**[Deer Getter](https://github.com/arc09walt/DeerGetter)** — a
Michigan roadkill-salvage board — runs on a porch raised by
this exact walk. The server stands on its operator's own PC,
behind its own fence (a tunnel door, no open ports, an operator
key, rate limits, plain-words logs and rattle alerts), and it
has been running live since November 2026. The guide points at
that board the way an apprentice's handbook points at the
master's own house: *this is what the road builds.*

## Honest limits

- **The guide walks one recipe**: a small server — the porch —
  on a Windows PC. Other machines and bigger servers are roads
  it doesn't walk yet, and it says so inside, in a list of
  unwalked paths. Honesty over all.
- **"From anywhere" means from anywhere once the street door
  is open.** The walk lights the porch inside your house —
  your PC, your phone, your network. Reaching it from the
  wider internet rides on the porch's street door: a tunnel,
  opened in the porch's own later pass. On the proof porch
  that pass is DONE: its street door stands open today, and
  the board answers from the street. The twelve stages alone
  don't put you on the internet — the guide says exactly
  this inside, under its unwalked paths.
- **The guide does nothing by itself.** It executes no
  commands, installs nothing, and changes no settings. Every
  consequential step is your press, on your machine. If you
  want a program that does it all for you, this isn't it — on
  purpose.
- **Your server is up when your PC is up.** A home server has
  a home server's hours. The walk doesn't pretend otherwise.
